# I Made an LLM Lay Siege to My Minecraft House
# 我让大语言模型围攻了我的《我的世界》基地

Can a language model do live adversarial-level design? Yes, emphasis on the adversarial part.
大语言模型能进行实时的对抗性关卡设计吗？可以，重点在于“对抗性”这部分。

The final ray of light fades behind the horizon — dusk. You shift around anxiously, fiddling with your sword and bow — in this world, you never know which night will be your last. Before you can even finish contemplating your fate, you hear it — a distant explosion, followed by a faint rumbling — they’re here. Creepers. Let’s pray your core, surrounded by obsidian and water, will last the night.
最后一缕光线在地平线后消逝——黄昏降临。你焦虑地挪动着脚步，摆弄着手中的剑和弓——在这个世界里，你永远不知道哪一个夜晚会是你的最后一夜。还没等你来得及思考自己的命运，你就听到了声音——远处的爆炸声，紧接着是轻微的轰鸣——它们来了。苦力怕。祈祷吧，愿你那被黑曜石和水环绕的核心能撑过今晚。

Hello! Back to reality — overly dramatic hook aside, the point I’m trying to make is pretty simple. Imagine a Minecraft player is building their base: they dig a moat, make walls, build some static defenses — and all the while, a malevolent god, fed some descriptions of the base, is looking to send them their next wave of enemies. Zombies that place sponges to drain the moat, followed by creepers to blow a hole in the walls, and spiders that flood in after. Nobody scripted that encounter! Nothing in the mod knows what a moat is or that sponges beat water.
你好！回到现实——抛开那些过于戏剧化的开场白，我想表达的观点其实很简单。想象一下，一个《我的世界》玩家正在建造基地：他们挖了护城河，筑起围墙，建立了一些静态防御设施——而与此同时，一位邪恶的神明在接收了基地描述后，正盘算着向他们派遣下一波敌人。僵尸放置海绵来排干护城河，紧接着苦力怕炸开围墙，随后蜘蛛蜂拥而入。没有人编写过这样的遭遇战脚本！模组里没有任何东西知道什么是护城河，也不知道海绵能克制水。

Today we’re going to see if a language model can look at a described situation, figure out why the defense worked, and compose a new wave to exploit it. That’s the premise of today’s article, and the question underneath it: can a language model do adversarial level design? Can it read a specific opponent, understand what’s keeping them alive, and counter it?
今天，我们将看看大语言模型是否能观察描述的情境，找出防御奏效的原因，并构思出一波新的攻势来破解它。这就是本文的前提，也是其背后的核心问题：大语言模型能进行对抗性关卡设计吗？它能解读特定的对手，理解是什么在维持他们的生存，并进行反制吗？

But before any of that, it’s worth looking at how games have done this for the last fifteen years, because the whole idea of a “director” is older than the LLMs I’m throwing at it rather irresponsibly. I mean a Game Director as in a state machine/game system, not like a guy making the game.
但在这一切开始之前，有必要回顾一下过去十五年里游戏是如何处理这一点的，因为“导演（Director）”这个概念比我正在不负责任地尝试使用的这些大语言模型要古老得多。我所说的“游戏导演”是指一种状态机或游戏系统，而不是指制作游戏的人。

Adversarial/adaptive difficulty is somewhat of an old-game dream and current half-reality. A game pushing back dynamically against player actions can heighten immersion and prevent exploits (i.e. if your players are hiding in one corner mowing down weaklings). The classic version of this is an AI Director! If you’ve ever played *Left 4 Dead*, you’ve met a director. Instead of placing the zombies in fixed spawn locations, the director “places enemies in varying positions and numbers based upon each player’s current situation, status, skill, and location.”
对抗性/自适应难度在某种程度上是老游戏的梦想，也是当前的半现实。游戏根据玩家行为动态反击可以增强沉浸感并防止利用漏洞（例如，如果玩家躲在一个角落里屠杀弱小的敌人）。这种机制的经典版本就是“AI导演”！如果你玩过《求生之路》，你就见过这种导演。导演不是将僵尸放置在固定的出生点，而是“根据每个玩家当前的情况、状态、技能和位置，在不同的位置放置不同数量的敌人。”

Despite the complicated-sounding description, the internals are pretty straightforward. The Director tracks an “emotional intensity” per survivor from 0 to 1 — it climbs when infected attack or die near you and decays during calm periods. The director watches that value and walks through a state machine — build up, peak, then relax — making a “sawtooth-esque” loop that keeps the game feeling alive.
尽管描述听起来很复杂，但其内部逻辑相当直观。导演会追踪每位幸存者的“情绪强度”（0到1之间）——当感染者在附近攻击或死亡时，数值会上升；在平静时期则会衰减。导演观察这个数值并运行一个状态机——积蓄、高潮、放松——形成一个“锯齿状”循环，让游戏保持活力。

There’s *Risk of Rain 2*. Its Combat Directors accumulate “credits” that grow over time and periodically spend them to buy a group of up to four enemies off a priced menu. A weak Lemurian is pocket change; a Magma Worm boss is the top shelf; slap an elite modifier on something and the price multiplies. So the Director saves up, then blows its whole wallet at once, which is why RoR2’s pressure arrives in sudden lurches rather than a steady drip.
再看看《雨中冒险2》。它的战斗导演会随时间积累“点数”，并定期花费这些点数从菜单中购买最多四个敌人。弱小的狐猴只是零钱；岩浆蠕虫BOSS是顶级商品；给敌人加上精英词缀，价格就会成倍增长。因此，导演会积攒点数，然后一次性花光，这就是为什么《雨中冒险2》的压力是突然爆发的，而不是持续不断的。

But neither does… *L4D* boils you down to a stress value. *RoR2* boils the whole game down to a bank balance. Both shipped to millions and both play great, but there’s a whole layer they just don’t have. Neither one can look at a water moat, or a one-tile choke you’re funneling everything through, or the pillar you’re kiting laps around, and go “ah, that’s what’s keeping them alive, here’s how I take it away.”
但它们都做不到一件事……《求生之路》把你简化为一个压力值，《雨中冒险2》把整个游戏简化为一个银行余额。两者都卖出了数百万份且体验极佳，但它们都缺失了一个层面。它们都无法观察到护城河、你用来卡位的单格通道，或是你绕圈风筝的柱子，然后意识到：“啊，这就是让他们存活的原因，我该如何破解它。”

A moat is not a number. Figuring out that someone is leaning on water to break your melee pathing — and that you could answer it by draining the water or just bringing something that ignores it — takes actually understanding the fort. Scalars can’t understand a fort.
护城河不是一个数字。要意识到有人利用水来破坏近战寻路，并想到通过排干水或带上能无视水的敌人来反制，这需要真正理解这座堡垒。标量数值无法理解堡垒。

If that wasn’t an obvious segue, that’s the gap. So the bet behind *Attack of the B(ot) Team*: hand a model a description of a defense it has never seen, and see whether it can do what the scalar directors structurally can’t.
如果这还不够明显，这就是差距所在。因此，《Attack of the B(ot) Team》背后的赌注是：给模型一份它从未见过的防御描述，看看它是否能做到标量导演在结构上无法做到的事情。
