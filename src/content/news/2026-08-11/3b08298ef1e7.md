# Deep Evidential Regression for Sparse Forest Height Estimation from Multimodal Satellite Imagery
# 基于多模态卫星影像的稀疏森林高度估计：深度证据回归方法

**Abstract:** Accurate estimation of forest height from satellite imagery is essential for applications such as carbon accounting, biodiversity monitoring, and ecosystem management. While recent deep learning approaches provide accurate predictions, they typically do not quantify predictive uncertainty. This limitation is particularly relevant in geospatial settings characterized by sparse supervision and geographic distribution shift.

**摘要：** 从卫星影像中准确估算森林高度对于碳核算、生物多样性监测和生态系统管理等应用至关重要。尽管近期的深度学习方法能够提供准确的预测，但它们通常无法量化预测的不确定性。在以稀疏监督和地理分布偏移为特征的地理空间场景中，这一局限性尤为突出。

In this work, we investigate Deep Evidential Regression (DER) for forest height estimation on the TreeUQ benchmark, a large-scale dataset designed for the joint estimation of tree count and average tree height at 10 m resolution, based on Sentinel-1/-2 data as well as tree inventory data over the federal state of Bavaria. To account for the extreme label sparsity of the tree inventory data, we introduce a masked evidential loss for dense geospatial prediction.

在这项工作中，我们研究了深度证据回归（DER）在 TreeUQ 基准测试上的森林高度估计表现。TreeUQ 是一个大规模数据集，旨在基于 Sentinel-1/-2 数据以及德国巴伐利亚州的树木清单数据，以 10 米分辨率联合估算树木数量和平均树高。为了解决树木清单数据中极端的标签稀疏性问题，我们引入了一种用于密集地理空间预测的掩码证据损失函数（masked evidential loss）。

Using a U-Net architecture with multimodal Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 inputs, the proposed approach jointly predicts tree height and associated uncertainty estimates in a single forward pass. Experimental results show that DER achieves predictive performance comparable to a deterministic U-Net while additionally providing well-calibrated uncertainty estimates. These findings demonstrate the potential of evidential learning as an efficient framework for uncertainty-aware forest structure estimation from Earth observation data.

该方法采用结合多模态 Sentinel-1 和 Sentinel-2 输入的 U-Net 架构，在单次前向传播中同时预测树高及其相关的不确定性估计。实验结果表明，DER 在实现与确定性 U-Net 相当的预测性能的同时，还能提供校准良好的不确定性估计。这些发现证明了证据学习作为一种高效框架的潜力，可用于从地球观测数据中进行具备不确定性感知能力的森林结构估计。
