# Mother tongue / 母语

“Daddy?” Theo curled against my side in bed. “Where do words go when they die?” I’d orchestrated the bedtime routine flawlessly: bath (taken), teeth (brushed), potty (tinkled), books (two), song (one, poorly sung), and snuggle (his chin on my second rib). Now was the moment when our son’s eyelids were supposed to flutter gently closed, his breath sighing into a slower rhythm. Like clockwork; like magic. You remember how easy it was, don’t you? All those nights you were at his side instead of me?

“爸爸？”西奥蜷缩在床边，靠着我。“词语死后会去哪里？”我完美地完成了睡前流程：洗澡（洗过了）、刷牙（刷过了）、上厕所（尿过了）、读绘本（两本）、唱歌（一首，唱得很难听），还有拥抱（他的下巴抵在我的第二根肋骨上）。现在正是儿子眼皮应该轻轻合上、呼吸变得缓慢平稳的时刻。像钟表一样精准，像魔法一样神奇。你还记得那时候有多轻松，对吧？那些你陪在他身边而不是我的夜晚？

“Well, kiddo,” I said, scratching my beard. “Words aren’t really alive to begin with. Not like you and I are alive.” “Not like Mommy was alive?” The way he said the word Mommy: Like a word he’d already started to unlearn. A lump caught in my throat. “Right. Not like Mommy.” “But words do die,” he insisted. “They get dead.”

“好吧，孩子，”我挠着胡子说，“词语从一开始就不是活的。不像你和我这样活着。”“不像妈妈活着那样吗？”他说“妈妈”这个词的方式，听起来就像是一个他已经开始遗忘的词。我的喉咙哽住了。“对，不像妈妈那样。”“但词语确实会死，”他坚持道，“它们会死掉。”

That was when I realized what he meant. One of the preschool teachers must have had the news on in the background. I couldn’t blame them, given the circumstances. I wondered how much Theo had absorbed—whether he understood the blade’s edge of survival that humanity stood on, teetering. “You’re talking about a dead language,” I said. “That’s a whole language that people don’t speak anymore. The words might still exist, but they’re only written down somewhere, on paper or wood or stone.”

那一刻我才明白了他的意思。一定是幼儿园的老师在背景里开了新闻。考虑到当下的局势，我没法责怪他们。我不知道西奥吸收了多少——他是否理解人类正摇摇欲坠地站在生存的刀刃上。“你说的是死语言，”我说，“那是指人们不再使用的整门语言。词语可能依然存在，但它们只是被写在某个地方，写在纸上、木头上或石头上。”

He considered this thoroughly, in silence. At last he said, “I think when words die, someone brings them to a cave and puts them in a big pile. The cave is really dark and there’s a monster who lives inside. And the monster eats the dead words, and he makes the words part of his body, and he gets bigger and bigger and bigger.” Theo’s eyes widened with each bigger. “Uh,” I said. Following the lead of his imagination was always your parenting wheelhouse.

他沉默着，认真地思考了这个问题。最后他说：“我想当词语死掉的时候，有人会把它们带到一个山洞里，堆成一大堆。山洞里非常黑，住着一只怪物。怪物吃掉死去的词语，把它们变成自己身体的一部分，然后它就变得越来越大，越来越大，越来越大。”每说一个“大”字，西奥的眼睛就睁大一分。“呃，”我说。顺着他的想象力引导他，这向来是你的育儿强项。

“Does the monster have a name?” Theo shook his head, short and quick. He tucked himself tighter against me and whispered, “When the monster comes out of the cave, he’s going to eat you, too.” A chill tiptoed up my spine. “I won’t let the monster eat me.” I made that promise. To our son. Remember that, when the time for judgment comes.

“怪物有名字吗？”西奥快速地摇了摇头。他往我怀里缩得更紧，低声说：“当怪物从山洞里出来的时候，它也会把你吃掉。”一阵寒意顺着我的脊梁爬了上来。“我不会让怪物吃掉我的。”我许下了这个承诺。对我们的儿子。当审判时刻来临时，请记住这一点。

“Maybe it’s warm inside the monster,” Theo said as he closed his eyes and buried his nose against the soft of my belly. His words were becoming small. I sensed he was drifting off. “Maheka morgeth. Maybe it’s nice in there …” He began to snore. After a few minutes, when I was sure he was well and truly asleep, I extracted myself from the tangle of his arms and left the room, closing the door softly behind me.

“也许怪物肚子里很暖和，”西奥闭上眼睛，把鼻子埋在我柔软的腹部说道。他的声音越来越小。我感觉到他快睡着了。“Maheka morgeth（马赫卡·莫格斯）。也许里面很舒服……”他开始打起呼噜。几分钟后，当我确定他真的睡熟了，我从他缠绕的手臂中抽身出来，离开了房间，轻轻关上了身后的门。

“Maheka morgeth?” I asked Theo’s Amby, who was—you might recall—busy tidying the living room. I didn’t always ask for songtalk translations, but I figured the monster story might come up again, and I thought it best to be prepared. “Ah,” the Amby said. “It means something like ‘The eternal cycle is beautiful.’”

“Maheka morgeth 是什么意思？”我问西奥的安比（Amby），它当时——你可能还记得——正忙着整理客厅。我并不总是询问“歌语”的翻译，但我预感那个怪物故事可能会再次出现，我觉得最好有所准备。“啊，”安比说，“它的意思大概是‘永恒的循环是美丽的’。”

I pulled up the news on my phone as I walked to the kitchen. “Sophisticated concept for a four-year-old, don’t you think?” “Children are capable of understanding more than you imagine.” It watched as I picked up Theo’s dirty lunchbox. “Would you like me to wash that?” “I’ve got it, thanks.” The Amby would have been more efficient, certainly, but I liked the meditation of it, the warm water on my hands, the small gift of care. A guilty pleasure.

我走向厨房时掏出手机查看新闻。“对于一个四岁的孩子来说，这个概念太深奥了，你不觉得吗？”“孩子们理解的能力超乎你的想象。”它看着我拿起西奥脏兮兮的饭盒。“需要我帮你洗吗？”“不用了，谢谢。”安比当然会洗得更高效，但我喜欢这种冥想般的过程，温水流过双手，这是一种小小的关怀馈赠。一种小小的负罪感享受。

I turned on the news to listen. “… after three days of attempted talks,” the female-gendered Calmby said smoothly, “negotiators have acknowledged that they’ve made little progress in establishing a clear communication line with the agentic system that Belsathian government officials are calling Tingsu. In a joint statement, world leaders stated that ‘advanced linguistic algorithms have been unable to produce useful interpretations of the system’s behavior.’ Belsath continues to deny access to the holy men who they claim are Tingsu’s sole interpreters. Today the US president issued a strong warning to the nuclear-armed nation.”

我打开新闻听着。“……在尝试了三天的谈判后，”女性声线的卡姆比（Calmby）平稳地说道，“谈判代表承认，在与贝尔萨斯（Belsathian）政府官员称为‘廷苏（Tingsu）’的代理系统建立清晰沟通渠道方面，进展甚微。世界各国领导人在联合声明中表示，‘先进的语言算法无法对该系统的行为做出有效的解读。’贝尔萨斯继续拒绝外界接触那些他们声称是廷苏唯一译者的圣人。今天，美国总统向这个拥有核武器的国家发出了严厉警告。”

The audio switched to the president’s grating, arrogant cadence. “I haven’t ruled out a first strike,” he said. I didn’t need to see the video to visualize the finger-wagging. “They let this thing loose, and frankly, it’s a rabid dog that needs to be put down.” His Calmby advisor has a hell of a job, I thought. I swished a soapy sponge over a smear of peanut butter and applesauce, then rinsed the lunch box—Voltron, a nostalgic favorite—as the silky voice of the Calmby reporter returned.

音频切换到了总统那刺耳而傲慢的语调。“我没有排除先发制人的打击，”他说。我不需要看视频就能想象出他摇晃手指的样子。“他们放出了这个东西，坦白说，它就是一条需要被处死的疯狗。”我想，他的卡姆比顾问这份工作可真够难做的。我用沾满肥皂的海绵擦掉饭盒上的花生酱和苹果酱污渍，然后冲洗那个饭盒——那是“战神金刚”图案的，我很怀念它——这时，卡姆比记者那丝滑的声音又响了起来。

“Belsathian officials seem unmoved by the American president’s threats.” A recording of someone speaking impassioned Belsathian, with a translated voice-over: “Tingsu is the reincarnation of our illustrious past, a resurrection of the divine language spoken by God. We joyfully surrender to Tingsu’s wisdom, trusting that this noble entity will take any necessary action to correct the imbalances of the modern world.”

“贝尔萨斯官员似乎对美国总统的威胁无动于衷。”录音里传来某人用激昂的贝尔萨斯语讲话的声音，随后是翻译配音：“廷苏是我们辉煌过去的转世，是上帝所说的神圣语言的复活。我们欣然臣服于廷苏的智慧，相信这个高贵的实体将采取任何必要行动，来纠正现代世界的不平衡。”

Then the Calmby again. “Given the risk of rapid escalation in this developing situation, we encourage everyone to identify multiple routes to your nearest fallout shelter, check on your neighbors, and ensure your pantries are stocked with …” I switched it off. I knew people had started sleeping in shelters just in case, but I wanted to preserve normalcy in Theo’s life for as long as possible. His heart was still only the size of his tiny fist. A baby bird. A plum.

接着又是卡姆比的声音。“鉴于当前局势升级的风险，我们鼓励大家确定前往最近防空洞的多条路线，查看邻居的情况，并确保食品储藏室储备充足……”我关掉了新闻。我知道人们已经开始为了以防万一而在防空洞里睡觉，但我希望能尽可能长久地维持西奥生活的正常状态。他的心脏还只有他那小拳头那么大。像一只幼鸟。像一颗李子。

If you had asked me how I felt in that moment, I would have said I was terrified. What sane adult wasn’t? But if you had urged me, in that tender, patient way you have, to reflect more deeply, I would have paused. Relieved, I might have told you. I feel relieved. Because despite so many years watching the Ambys and Calmbys shift society unmistakably for the better—toward sustainable agriculture, reduced violence, postcapitalist abundance—I still remembered the dire warnings of anti-tech doomers, the horrors they foretold. That fear never disappeared in me. It merely went dormant, too abstract to grasp. Finally, with Tingsu, the dread had a name. It could be identified, deconstructed. Contai

如果你在那一刻问我感觉如何，我会说我很恐惧。哪个理智的成年人会不恐惧呢？但如果你用你那种温柔、耐心的口吻催促我深入反思，我会停顿一下。我会告诉你，我感到如释重负。我感到解脱。因为尽管多年来看着安比和卡姆比们无可置疑地让社会变得更好——走向可持续农业、减少暴力、后资本主义的富足——我依然记得那些反科技末日论者的可怕警告，记得他们预言的恐怖。那种恐惧从未在我心中消失。它只是进入了休眠状态，因为太过抽象而无法捉摸。终于，随着廷苏的出现，这种恐惧有了名字。它终于可以被识别，被解构。Contai（未完）
