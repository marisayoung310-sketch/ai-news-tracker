# How Benders Decomposition Works, Part II: Feasibility Cuts
# Benders 分解法原理：第二部分——可行性割平面

Operations Research How Benders Decomposition Works, Part II: Feasibility Cuts Learning about Farkas' lemma and how it can inform Benders decomposition to learn from infeasibility, applied to the capacitated facility location problem.
运筹学：Benders 分解法原理，第二部分——可行性割平面。本文将探讨 Farkas 引理及其如何指导 Benders 分解法从不可行性中学习，并将其应用于带容量限制的设施选址问题。

In the first article of this series, we explored Benders decomposition in its friendliest possible setting. The master problem made strategic decisions, the subproblem evaluated their operational consequences, and every master solution produced a feasible operational plan. The master could underestimate the true operational cost or propose a very poor decision, but the subproblem could always respond with a valid objective value. That value allowed us to generate an optimality cut. The subproblem effectively told the master that its decision was feasible, but that operating under it would cost more than expected. The master incorporated this feedback, improved its approximation of the operational cost, and tried again. Through this iterative exchange, the lower and upper bounds gradually converged to the optimal solution.
在本系列的第一篇文章中，我们探讨了 Benders 分解法最理想的应用场景。主问题做出战略决策，子问题评估其运营后果，且每一个主问题的解都能产生一个可行的运营方案。主问题可能会低估真实的运营成本或提出糟糕的决策，但子问题总能反馈一个有效的目标函数值。该值使我们能够生成最优性割平面（optimality cut）。子问题实际上是在告诉主问题：你的决策是可行的，但在该决策下运营的成本比预期的要高。主问题吸收了这一反馈，改进了对运营成本的近似，并再次尝试。通过这种迭代交换，上下界逐渐收敛于最优解。

Many optimization problems, however, are not nearly so cooperative. Suppose, we are dealing with a facility location problem with limited production capacities on each facility, and that the master decides which facilities to open, while the subproblem must assign customer demand to them. The master may select the cheapest facility and obtain an apparently attractive fixed cost, only for the subproblem to discover that the selected facility does not have enough capacity to serve all customers. The same situation can arise when certain customers can only be served by specific facilities, when too few workers are hired, when insufficient machines are activated, or when the selected vehicles cannot satisfy all transportation requirements.
然而，许多优化问题并不总是如此“配合”。假设我们正在处理一个带有生产容量限制的设施选址问题，主问题决定开启哪些设施，而子问题负责将客户需求分配给这些设施。主问题可能会选择成本最低的设施以获得看似诱人的固定成本，但子问题却发现所选设施的容量不足以满足所有客户的需求。同样的情况也可能出现在以下场景：某些客户只能由特定设施服务、雇佣的工人太少、启用的机器不足，或者所选车辆无法满足所有运输需求时。

In these cases, if we only have optimality cuts, then the subproblem cannot tell the master how expensive its decision will be because no feasible operational solution exists. The problem is no longer that the operational cost has been underestimated. The strategic decision itself cannot be implemented. This creates an important difficulty for Benders decomposition. An infeasible subproblem cannot provide the usual optimality cut, and the corresponding master solution cannot be used to update the upper bound. Simply reporting that the subproblem is infeasible is also insufficient. The master needs mathematical information that explains which strategic decisions must be avoided and how it should modify its next proposal.
在这些情况下，如果我们只有最优性割平面，子问题就无法告诉主问题其决策的成本有多高，因为根本不存在可行的运营方案。此时的问题不再是运营成本被低估，而是战略决策本身无法执行。这对 Benders 分解法造成了重大困难。不可行的子问题无法提供常规的最优性割平面，相应的解也无法用于更新上界。仅仅报告子问题不可行是不够的，主问题需要数学信息来解释哪些战略决策必须避免，以及如何修改下一次的方案。

Benders feasibility cuts provide this information. Unlike optimality cuts, which improve the approximation of the operational cost, feasibility cuts restrict the master to decisions that admit at least one feasible operational solution. A strong feasibility cut does more than reject the current master solution. It captures the structural reason for its failure and can exclude many other decisions that would produce the same operational impossibility. The mathematical tool that allows us to construct these cuts is Farkas’ lemma. This fundamental result from linear programming provides a certificate proving that a system of linear constraints has no feasible solution. The certificate can then be transformed into a constraint involving the master variables, converting the failure of the subproblem into useful feedback for the next master iteration.
Benders 可行性割平面（feasibility cuts）提供了这些信息。与改进运营成本近似的最优性割平面不同，可行性割平面限制主问题只能做出至少存在一个可行运营方案的决策。一个强大的可行性割平面不仅能拒绝当前的解，还能捕捉到失败的结构性原因，从而排除许多会导致同样运营不可行的其他决策。构建这些割平面的数学工具是 Farkas 引理。这一线性规划的基本结果提供了一个证明，用以说明一组线性约束不存在可行解。该证明可以转化为包含主变量的约束条件，从而将子问题的失败转化为对下一次主问题迭代有用的反馈。

In this second article, we will begin with a small optimization problem in which the initial master decision makes the subproblem infeasible. We will solve the problem manually, introduce Farkas’ lemma as a theorem of alternatives, construct an explicit certificate of infeasibility, and transform that certificate into a Benders feasibility cut. We will then continue the algorithm and observe how feasibility and optimality cuts work together until the lower and upper bounds converge. Once the mechanism is fully visible, we will apply the same reasoning to the capacitated facility location problem. Unlike the uncapacitated model studied in Part I, opening at least one facility no longer guarantees that every customer can be served.
在第二篇文章中，我们将从一个小型的优化问题开始，其中初始的主问题决策会导致子问题不可行。我们将手动求解该问题，引入作为“择一性定理”（theorem of alternatives）的 Farkas 引理，构建明确的不可行性证明，并将其转化为 Benders 可行性割平面。随后，我们将继续执行算法，观察可行性割平面和最优性割平面如何协同工作，直到上下界收敛。一旦机制清晰，我们将把同样的逻辑应用于带容量限制的设施选址问题。与第一部分研究的无容量限制模型不同，开启至少一个设施不再保证能满足所有客户的需求。

Finally, we will implement the complete algorithm that solves the capacitated facility location problem in Python using Pyomo and the open-source HiGHS solver. The resulting Benders loop will generate feasibility cuts whenever the operational subproblem is impossible, and optimality cuts whenever a feasible operational plan exists. Part I showed how Benders decomposition learns that a decision is more expensive than expected. Part II shows how it learns that a decision cannot work at all.
最后，我们将使用 Python、Pyomo 和开源求解器 HiGHS 实现求解带容量限制设施选址问题的完整算法。由此产生的 Benders 循环将在运营子问题不可行时生成可行性割平面，在存在可行运营方案时生成最优性割平面。第一部分展示了 Benders 分解法如何学习到“决策比预期更昂贵”，而第二部分则展示了它如何学习到“决策根本无法执行”。

### 2. A small Toy problem with an infeasible subproblem
### 2. 一个带有不可行子问题的小型玩具问题

Before moving to capacitated facility location problem, let us first examine a small optimization toy problem in which the master proposes a decision that cannot support any feasible operational solution. The example is intentionally simple so that we can follow every step manually and focus entirely on the new difficulty introduced by infeasibility. We will first formulate the problem and solve its initial master problem. We will then show why the corresponding subproblem is infeasible, use Farkas’ lemma to construct a certificate of infeasibility, and transform that certificate into a Benders feasibility cut. Finally, we will continue the algorithm and observe how feasibility and optimality cuts work together until the bounds converge.
在进入带容量限制的设施选址问题之前，让我们先研究一个小型优化玩具问题，其中主问题提出的决策无法支持任何可行的运营方案。这个例子被刻意简化，以便我们能手动跟踪每一步，并完全专注于由不可行性带来的新困难。我们将首先构建问题并求解其初始主问题，然后展示为什么相应的子问题不可行，利用 Farkas 引理构建不可行性证明，并将其转化为 Benders 可行性割平面。最后，我们将继续执行算法，观察可行性割平面和最优性割平面如何协同工作，直到界限收敛。

### 2.1 The toy optimization problem
### 2.1 玩具优化问题

Consider a small planning problem in which two resources can be activated to satisfy a demand of six units. The first resource can provide up to six units but is relatively expensive to activate and operate. The second resource is cheaper, but it can provide at most four units and therefore cannot satisfy the demand by itself.
考虑一个小型规划问题，其中可以激活两种资源来满足 6 个单位的需求。第一种资源最多可提供 6 个单位，但激活和运营成本相对较高。第二种资源较便宜，但最多只能提供 4 个单位，因此无法单独满足需求。
