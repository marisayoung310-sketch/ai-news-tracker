### This company’s plans to deploy space mirrors could jeopardize the night sky for many
### 这家公司部署太空镜的计划可能会破坏许多人的夜空

A company that plans to beam sunlight from space to Earth on demand might unintentionally brighten the night sky for many more people than intended, according to a new study. Later this year, Reflect Orbital plans to launch a test satellite that will extend an 18-by-18-meter mirror in orbit. The goal is to eventually launch up to 50,000 larger satellites that can reflect sunlight to Earth on demand. Reflect Orbital says the technology could extend sunlight for solar panel charging, emergency response, and military activities. But new research suggests the giant beams could shine as bright as 10,000 full moons and scatter light over tens of kilometers, raising concerns about dark skies, aviation, and wildlife. Read the full story on the fierce debate over the space mirrors. —Jonathan O'Callaghan
一项新研究显示，一家计划按需将阳光从太空反射到地球的公司，可能会在无意中让比预期多得多的人面临夜空变亮的问题。今年晚些时候，Reflect Orbital 公司计划发射一颗测试卫星，在轨道上展开一面 18x18 米的镜子。其目标是最终发射多达 50,000 颗更大的卫星，按需将阳光反射回地球。Reflect Orbital 表示，这项技术可以延长光照时间，用于太阳能电池板充电、应急响应和军事活动。但最新研究表明，这些巨大的光束亮度可能相当于 10,000 轮满月，并将光线散射到数十公里外，引发了人们对暗夜保护、航空安全和野生动物生存环境的担忧。阅读关于太空镜激烈辩论的完整报道。——Jonathan O'Callaghan

### When AI designs a drug, who gets the credit?
### 当人工智能设计出药物时，谁该获得功劳？

When Insilico Medicine used its computer models to propose a promising drug for pulmonary fibrosis, it enthusiastically claimed that the molecule had been “discovered by” generative AI. But when it filed a patent, the company made no mention of AI. Instead, it named five humans as the drug’s “inventors.” The discrepancy points to a fascinating wrinkle in intellectual-property law. No matter how fundamental an AI is to a discovery, when it comes to winning rights to an invention, it’s humans—and only humans—who can take the credit. As AI models become capable of generating drug designs as easily as ChatGPT can write a thank-you note, the question of who actually invented something could get much harder to answer. Here’s how AI drug discovery could force patent law to confront what it means to invent. —Antonio Regalado
当英矽智能（Insilico Medicine）利用其计算机模型提出一种治疗肺纤维化的潜力药物时，该公司热情地宣称该分子是“由生成式人工智能发现的”。但在申请专利时，该公司却只字未提人工智能，而是将五名人类列为该药物的“发明人”。这种差异揭示了知识产权法中一个引人入胜的矛盾点：无论人工智能在发现过程中发挥了多么基础的作用，在争取发明权利时，只有人类才能获得署名权。随着人工智能模型生成药物设计变得像 ChatGPT 写感谢信一样简单，关于“谁才是真正的发明者”这一问题将变得愈发难以回答。以下是人工智能药物发现如何迫使专利法重新审视“发明”定义的分析。——Antonio Regalado

**Meet the new biologists treating LLMs like aliens**
**认识一下那些把大语言模型当作外星生物研究的新生物学家**

LLMs are such vast and complicated systems that nobody quite understands what they are, how they work, or what they can really do—not even the people who build them. That makes it hard to get a grip on their hallucinations, set up effective guardrails, and know when to trust them. So researchers are trying something different: studying them as if they were doing biology or neuroscience on vast living creatures—city-size xenomorphs that have appeared in our midst. They’re discovering that large language models are even weirder than they thought. But they also now have a clearer sense than ever of what these models can and can’t do—and what’s going on under the hood when they do unexpected things. Step inside the strange new science of understanding LLMs. —Will Douglas Heaven
大语言模型（LLM）是如此庞大而复杂的系统，以至于没有人能完全理解它们是什么、如何运作，或者它们真正能做什么——即使是构建它们的人也不例外。这使得人们很难掌控它们的“幻觉”、设置有效的护栏，以及判断何时该信任它们。因此，研究人员正在尝试一种不同的方法：像研究生物学或神经科学一样，将它们视为巨大的生物——即出现在我们中间的城市规模的异形。他们发现，大语言模型比他们想象的还要怪异。但他们现在也比以往任何时候都更清楚这些模型能做什么、不能做什么，以及当它们做出意想不到的行为时，其内部究竟发生了什么。走进理解大语言模型的奇妙新科学。——Will Douglas Heaven
