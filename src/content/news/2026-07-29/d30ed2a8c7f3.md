# Can the New York Times Save Journalism From Our AI Overlords?
# 《纽约时报》能从人工智能巨头手中拯救新闻业吗？

For most companies, spending upwards of $20 million on a single legal fight with an uncertain outcome might mean the boss has some explaining to do. For A.G. Sulzberger, the publisher of The New York Times, that explanation is simple: It’s the cost of defending journalism—at the Times and everywhere else.
对于大多数公司而言，在一场结果不确定的法律诉讼上花费超过 2000 万美元，可能意味着老板需要给出一番解释。但对于《纽约时报》发行人 A.G. 苏尔茨伯格（A.G. Sulzberger）来说，这个解释很简单：这是捍卫新闻业的代价——不仅是为了《纽约时报》，也是为了整个行业。

Sulzberger, who took on the publisher role about a decade ago and has been an influential leader in the Times’ much-lauded push into digital innovation, has no shortage of defending to do lately. Nearly three years ago, the Times sued OpenAI and Microsoft for copyright infringement, alleging that the companies illegally used New York Times journalism to train AI models. That legal case, in which motions are still being filed about discovery requests, is only one front in a broader battle: The Trump administration is waging an ongoing war on press freedoms—most recently with an outrageous attempt to subpoena Times journalists over coverage of the president’s Qatar-gifted jet to be used as Air Force One—while AI companies radically reshape the way people find and consume information online.
苏尔茨伯格在大约十年前担任发行人，一直是《纽约时报》备受赞誉的数字化创新转型中的重要领导者，他最近需要捍卫的事情可不少。近三年前，《纽约时报》起诉 OpenAI 和微软侵犯版权，指控这些公司非法使用《纽约时报》的新闻内容来训练人工智能模型。这起法律案件目前仍处于证据开示请求的动议阶段，但这只是更广泛战役的一条战线：特朗普政府正在对新闻自由发动持续的战争——最近的一次是试图传唤《纽约时报》记者，原因涉及对总统使用卡塔尔赠送的喷气式飞机作为“空军一号”的报道——与此同时，人工智能公司正在从根本上重塑人们在线查找和消费信息的方式。

The Times has arguably never been stronger as a business, having recently surpassed 13 million subscribers, and its newsroom is widely seen among journalists as one of the most stable and well-resourced in the business. Still, Sulzberger believes that journalism itself is facing myriad existential dilemmas—from an administration ready and willing to target reporters, from AI companies whose products depend on the very reporting they risk undermining, and media companies being demolished by the changes they’re unleashing on the open web.
《纽约时报》的商业实力可以说达到了前所未有的高度，最近订阅用户数已突破 1300 万，其新闻编辑室在新闻界被广泛视为最稳定、资源最充足的机构之一。尽管如此，苏尔茨伯格认为新闻业本身正面临着无数生存困境——来自随时准备针对记者的政府，来自那些产品依赖于其所破坏的新闻报道的人工智能公司，以及那些因开放网络变革而遭受重创的媒体公司。

I sat down with Sulzberger in late July at WIRED’s offices. We talked about that Times lawsuit, how and where AI is transforming reporting inside the newsroom, and why he thinks journalists and media executives need to team up—even while they continue to compete fiercely. You can read our entire conversation below, or listen to it here.
7 月下旬，我在《连线》（WIRED）杂志的办公室采访了苏尔茨伯格。我们讨论了那场针对 OpenAI 的诉讼、人工智能如何以及在何处改变新闻编辑室的报道工作，以及为什么他认为记者和媒体高管需要携手合作——即使他们之间依然存在激烈的竞争。您可以阅读下方的完整对话，或点击此处收听。

Thanks for starting there, because it’s obviously yet another disturbing escalation in the Trump administration's efforts to punish and undermine the free press in this country. First I should say, I have so much admiration for the reporters who have done this work and who are doing it under a different kind of pressure than we have seen in generations. I think that by any objective metric, this is the biggest effort to go after the press that we've seen from a White House probably since Woodrow Wilson, so in more than a century.
感谢你从这里开始，因为这显然是特朗普政府试图惩罚和破坏这个国家新闻自由的又一次令人不安的升级。首先我必须说，我非常钦佩那些完成这项工作的记者，他们是在几代人以来从未见过的压力下进行报道的。我认为，从任何客观标准来看，这可能是自伍德罗·威尔逊（Woodrow Wilson）以来，即一个多世纪以来，白宫对新闻界采取的最大规模的打压行动。

Right. When that occurred, a lot of people had questions about whether it could ever be secure enough to hold the President of the United States and ferry him around the world. 没错。当这件事发生时，许多人质疑它是否足够安全，能够搭载美国总统并带他环游世界。果不其然，我们的记者发现，在它的一次首航中，即第一次前往土耳其的海外旅行中，有人担心它应对威胁的装备不足，总统不得不乘坐原来的“空军一号”飞回。他最初否认了这一点。但我们揭露了真相，随后这一真相也得到了证实。

On one side this is objectively very rare—there was similar overreach, you may remember, in a Washington Post case some months ago, where they actually raided the reporter's home and seized her devices—[but] on the other [hand], this is building naturally off of what I think is a broader effort.
我的意思是，这极其不寻常。这非常罕见。在我任职期间——我担任这个职位大约十年了——在《纽约时报》经历过的最接近的比较发生在特朗普第一届政府时期，当时我们一些记者的记录被司法部秘密扣押。事实上，这促使司法部进行了一些旨在遏制此类滥用权力的改革。一方面，这在客观上非常罕见——你可能还记得几个月前《华盛顿邮报》的一起案件中也出现了类似的越权行为，他们实际上突袭了记者的家并扣押了她的设备——但另一方面，这自然是基于我认为更广泛的打压行动。
