### How the “censorship-industrial complex” is changing the internet and US policy —Eileen Guo
### “审查-工业复合体”如何改变互联网与美国政策 ——Eileen Guo

I first heard the term “censorship-industrial complex” on April 15, 2025. That’s when I got the tip that a small office in the US State Department, which focused on monitoring and countering foreign disinformation from the likes of Russia, Iran, and China, was facing imminent shutdown—the next day. And the reason? The office was accused of serving as the department’s central hub in the so-called censorship-industrial complex—a sprawling constellation of government agencies, academics, civil society groups, and Big Tech platforms allegedly conspiring to suppress conservative and populist speech online under the guise of combating disinformation. I broke the story on April 16. But for me, it was just the start of a deep reporting rabbit hole into an idea that had moved from the fringes of the right-wing internet into the Trump administration. For more on what the narrative means for the internet, read my story here.
我第一次听到“审查-工业复合体”这个词是在 2025 年 4 月 15 日。当时我得到消息，美国国务院一个专注于监测和反击来自俄罗斯、伊朗和中国等国境外虚假信息的办公室即将面临关闭——就在第二天。原因是什么？该办公室被指控是所谓“审查-工业复合体”的部门中心枢纽——这是一个由政府机构、学术界、民间社会团体和大型科技平台组成的庞大网络，据称它们打着打击虚假信息的幌子，密谋压制网上的保守派和民粹主义言论。我在 4 月 16 日报道了这则新闻。但对我来说，这只是深入挖掘这一概念的开始，它已经从右翼互联网的边缘进入了特朗普政府。想了解更多关于这一叙事对互联网意味着什么的信息，请点击此处阅读我的报道。

### MIT Technology Review Narrated: Montana’s plan to become an experimental medical hub just pushed forward
### 《麻省理工科技评论》播客：蒙大拿州成为实验性医疗中心的计划取得进展

At the end of July, any biotech company in Montana with an experimental drug gained a clear path to selling it to consumers. Companies whose drugs have been through preliminary testing—sometimes in as few as 10 healthy people—can pay $12,500 to apply to a newly established review board. Once approved, they can set their own prices and sell the drugs through experimental treatment clinics, the first of which is likely to open around the end of this year. Montana’s latest right-to-try legislation is unique. While similar laws elsewhere limit access to people with terminal illness, Montana’s system is theoretically open to anyone who gives informed consent and can pay. That includes people desperate for treatments for rare diseases. It also includes those interested in longevity and drugs pitched as preventive therapies. —Jessica Hamzelou
7 月底，蒙大拿州任何拥有实验性药物的生物技术公司都获得了一条向消费者销售药物的明确途径。那些药物经过初步测试（有时仅在 10 名健康人身上进行）的公司，可以支付 12,500 美元向一个新成立的审查委员会提出申请。一旦获得批准，他们就可以自行定价，并通过实验性治疗诊所销售这些药物，首批诊所预计将于今年年底左右开业。蒙大拿州最新的“尝试权”立法非常独特。虽然其他地方的类似法律将使用范围限制在绝症患者身上，但蒙大拿州的系统理论上向任何能够知情同意并支付费用的人开放。这包括那些急需罕见病治疗方案的人，也包括那些对长寿和作为预防性疗法推销的药物感兴趣的人。——Jessica Hamzelou

### One More Thing
### 补充一点

Are we ready to hand AI agents the keys? We’re starting to give AI agents real autonomy, and we’re not prepared for what could happen next. Any action that can be captured by text is potentially within the purview of AI agents—which is why they can cause so much mischief. “The great paradox of agents is that the very thing that makes them useful—that they’re able to accomplish a range of tasks—involves giving away control.”
我们准备好将控制权交给 AI 智能体了吗？我们开始赋予 AI 智能体真正的自主权，但我们并未准备好迎接接下来可能发生的事情。任何可以通过文本捕捉的操作都可能在 AI 智能体的权限范围内——这就是它们为何能造成如此多麻烦的原因。“智能体最大的悖论在于，使它们变得有用的特质——即它们能够完成一系列任务——本身就涉及放弃控制权。”
