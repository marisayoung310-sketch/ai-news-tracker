# The Moon's shadow raced across the heart of Spain, and I was there to see it
# 月影掠过西班牙腹地，我亲眼见证了这一刻

DURATÓN, Spain—I’ll say it: I’m hooked. Standing in the shadow of the Moon is one of the rarest opportunities to directly sense the mechanics of our Solar System, when the dance between three celestial bodies slides into perfect alignment. It’s a mathematical miracle whose rarity and grandeur are made possible by the relative sizes and distances of the Moon and the Sun from Earth. Not every planet is so lucky.

西班牙杜拉通（DURATÓN）——我得承认：我上瘾了。站在月影之下，是直接感受太阳系运行机制最难得的机会之一，那是三颗天体在舞动中达到完美对齐的时刻。这是一个数学上的奇迹，其稀有与壮丽得益于月球、太阳与地球之间相对的大小和距离。并非每一颗行星都有这样的幸运。

The Moon’s shadow crisscrossed the American heartland twice in less than seven years, first in 2017 and again in 2024. I was fortunate to witness both. The experiences left me too impatient to wait for the next total solar eclipse in the continental United States in far-off 2044. That’s what pulled me to the plains of north-central Spain last week. This land of wide open spaces, ancient churches and castles, and vast sunflower fields, all baking under the blinding August Sun, seemed like the perfect location to enter the path of totality. The ongoing drought in Spain has turned the country’s fields and forests into a tinderbox, but those conditions made for ideal eclipse viewing.

月影在不到七年的时间里两次横跨美国腹地，分别是在2017年和2024年。我有幸见证了这两次日全食。这些经历让我无法忍受等待到遥远的2044年，去迎接美国本土下一次日全食。这就是上周吸引我前往西班牙中北部平原的原因。这片土地拥有广阔的空间、古老的教堂和城堡，以及大片的向日葵田，在八月刺眼的阳光下炙烤着，看起来是进入全食带的绝佳地点。西班牙持续的干旱使该国的田野和森林变得极易起火，但这些条件却为观测日食提供了理想的环境。

I’m an eclipse neophyte. This was only my third, far short of the world record (35) held by American umbraphiles Glenn Schneider and John Beattie. I crossed paths with far more seasoned eclipse chasers in Spain, one boasting as many as 20 trips into totality.

我是一名日食“新手”。这只是我第三次观测，与美国日食爱好者格伦·施耐德（Glenn Schneider）和约翰·比蒂（John Beattie）保持的世界纪录（35次）相比还差得很远。我在西班牙遇到了许多经验丰富的日食追逐者，其中一人甚至有20次进入全食带的经历。

### Curating the experience
### 策划观测之旅

Anyone planning a trip to see a total solar eclipse needs to keep several things in mind. Have a backup plan. Ideally, you should have several backup plans. Cloud cover will ruin an eclipse, so if you’re set on seeing it, you need to be mobile on the big day. You should also plan for crowds and traffic and allow plenty of time to reach your viewing location. And, of course, be sure to take a pair of safe eclipse-viewing glasses.

任何计划去观看日全食的人都需要记住几件事。准备一个备用计划。理想情况下，你应该有几个备用计划。云层会毁掉日食观测，所以如果你决心要看到它，在观测当天就需要保持机动性。你还应该考虑到人群和交通状况，并预留充足的时间到达观测地点。当然，一定要带上一副安全的日食观测眼镜。

It is also worth scouting for the eclipse ahead of time. A friend and I settled on a rural location in Duratón, Spain, about 60 miles (100 kilometers) north of Madrid. Watching from there would give us a little more than 1.5 minutes of totality with a bucolic landscape anchored by a Romanesque church dating to the 12th or 13th century. We traveled there exactly 24 hours before the eclipse to get an idea of what to expect.

提前踩点观测地点也是值得的。我和一位朋友选定了西班牙杜拉通的一个乡村地点，距离马德里约60英里（100公里）。在那里观看，我们将获得超过1.5分钟的全食时间，背景是田园诗般的景观，以及一座建于12或13世纪的罗马式教堂。我们在日食发生前整整24小时到达那里，以了解现场情况。

The old Catholic church in Duratón had ample parking and was far enough from sizable towns to escape the largest crowds. But we weren’t alone as eclipse time neared last Wednesday. One hundred or more local residents, tourists, and photographers descended on the church grounds with cameras, telescopes, music, and their favorite libations.

杜拉通那座古老的天主教堂有充足的停车位，而且距离大城镇足够远，可以避开最拥挤的人群。但上周三临近日食时，我们并不孤单。一百多名当地居民、游客和摄影师带着相机、望远镜、音乐和他们最喜欢的饮品涌向了教堂广场。

The setting was, at the same time, ancient and modern, natural and human. The church was built next to a Visigothic cemetery with anthropomorphic rock-cut tombs. Up the hill, about 1,000 feet away, lay the ruins of the Roman city of Confloenta undergoing archaeological study. For a few moments, I thought of how those civilizations in this very spot might have reacted to the splendor—no, terror!—of the Sun suddenly going dark.

这个场景既古老又现代，既自然又人文。教堂建在一座西哥特墓地旁边，那里有拟人化的岩凿墓穴。在约1000英尺外的山坡上，坐落着正在进行考古研究的罗马城市康弗洛恩塔（Confloenta）遗址。在那一刻，我想到在同一个地点，那些古代文明面对太阳突然变暗的壮丽——不，是恐惧！——会有怎样的反应。

By then, it was showtime. Through eclipse glasses, we could see the Moon taking an ever-larger bite from the Sun until, finally, less than an hour before sunset, the lights went out. The church bells rang. The crowds cheered. The Sun’s turbulent outer atmosphere appeared as a ghostly white halo over Duratón’s centuries-old place of worship. Moments later, Sol’s chariot resumed its fiery march across the sky, just as modern science predicted. No omens, no fear—just another extraordinarily beautiful experience locked into memory. There’s room for more.

那时，表演开始了。透过日食眼镜，我们可以看到月球一点点吞噬太阳，直到日落前不到一小时，光线彻底消失。教堂钟声响起，人群欢呼。太阳动荡的外层大气在杜拉通这座百年教堂上方呈现出一圈幽灵般的白色光环。片刻之后，正如现代科学所预测的那样，太阳的战车恢复了它在天空中炽热的行进。没有预兆，没有恐惧——只有又一次被锁进记忆中的绝美体验。未来还有更多期待。
