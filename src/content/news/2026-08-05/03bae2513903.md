### Trump’s AI protectionism has come for robotics — James O’Donnell
### 特朗普的 AI 保护主义已延伸至机器人领域 —— James O’Donnell

Humanoid robots usually elicit more cringe than awe: They stumble, kick children, and despite advances are still worse at using their hands than my toddler. It’s a nascent industry, and such robots are more commonly seen in viral videos than real workplaces or homes.
人形机器人通常带来的尴尬多于敬畏：它们步履蹒跚、踢到孩子，尽管有所进步，但使用双手的能力甚至还不如我的幼儿。这是一个新兴行业，这类机器人更多出现在病毒式传播的视频中，而非真实的职场或家庭里。

It was a surprise, then, when last week the Federal Trade Commission issued a sweeping ban on foreign imports of advanced robots, including humanoids, quadrupeds, and wheeled robots. The decision should be understood not as another chapter in the old China trade playbook, but as evidence that the Trump administration is expanding its protection of the AI industry beyond today’s leading labs. It is now willing to step in on behalf of an emerging robotics sector that is still barely finding its footing. Read our analysis to understand the ban’s potential impact.
因此，当美国联邦贸易委员会（FTC）上周发布针对先进机器人（包括人形、四足和轮式机器人）的全面进口禁令时，令人感到意外。这一决定不应被简单理解为旧版对华贸易策略的延续，而应被视为特朗普政府正在将 AI 产业保护范围从当前的领先实验室进一步扩大。政府现在愿意为尚处于起步阶段的机器人行业提供干预。阅读我们的分析，了解该禁令的潜在影响。
