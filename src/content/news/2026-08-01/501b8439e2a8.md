# How Benders Decomposition Works Part I: Optimality Cuts

**Benders 分解法原理（第一部分）：最优性割平面**

A friendly introduction to one of the most powerful optimization techniques using the uncapacitated facility location problem.
这是一篇关于最强大的优化技术之一的友好入门指南，我们将通过无容量限制设施选址问题来展开介绍。

The Second World War confronted military planners with coordination problems on a scale that had rarely been imagined. Aircraft, personnel, fuel, equipment, training, and supplies had to be allocated across an immense system, often under severe time pressure and uncertainty. This environment accelerated the development of operations research, a discipline founded on the idea that complex decisions could be studied systematically through data, scientific reasoning, and mathematics rather than addressed solely through experience and intuition [1].
第二次世界大战使军事规划者面临着前所未有的协调难题。飞机、人员、燃料、设备、训练和物资必须在庞大的系统中进行分配，且往往处于极度的时间压力和不确定性之下。这种环境加速了运筹学的发展，该学科建立在一个理念之上：复杂的决策可以通过数据、科学推理和数学进行系统性研究，而不是仅仅依靠经验和直觉来解决 [1]。

George Dantzig was among the researchers immersed in this new world of large-scale planning. During the war, he worked for the United States Army Air Forces, where he contributed to the analysis and planning of military operations. After the war, the Air Force challenged him to help mechanize planning processes that had previously been performed largely by hand. In 1947, Dantzig formulated the general linear programming problem and developed the simplex method, providing a systematic procedure for finding the best allocation of limited resources among competing activities [2, 3].
乔治·丹齐格（George Dantzig）是投身于这一大规模规划新世界的众多研究人员之一。战争期间，他在美国陆军航空队工作，为军事行动的分析和规划做出了贡献。战后，空军向他提出挑战，希望他能帮助实现此前主要靠手工完成的规划流程的机械化。1947年，丹齐格提出了通用线性规划问题并开发了单纯形法，为在竞争性活动中寻找有限资源的最佳分配提供了一套系统化的程序 [2, 3]。

This research continued through Project SCOOP, the Scientific Computation of Optimal Programs, and later at the RAND Corporation, which became one of the principal centers for the early development of linear programming and mathematical optimization [2].
这项研究通过“SCOOP项目”（最优程序科学计算）得以延续，随后在兰德公司（RAND Corporation）继续开展，该公司后来成为线性规划和数学优化早期发展的主要中心之一 [2]。

This mathematical revolution eventually reached the Shell Laboratories in Amsterdam, where Jacques Benders began working in 1955. Together with his office mate, Guus Zoutendijk, he encountered difficult refinery-planning problems involving production decisions, material flows, processing resources, and costs. After visiting Shell’s Californian branch and learning about the potential of linear programming, they returned to the Netherlands and began implementing the simplex method for production planning in the refinery and petrochemical industries. Mathematical optimization promised to help coordinate industrial systems whose many interconnected decisions could easily overwhelm unaided human reasoning [4].
这场数学革命最终传到了阿姆斯特丹的壳牌实验室，雅克·本德斯（Jacques Benders）于1955年开始在那里工作。他和办公室同事古斯·祖滕代克（Guus Zoutendijk）遇到了棘手的炼油厂规划问题，涉及生产决策、物流、加工资源和成本。在访问了壳牌加州分公司并了解了线性规划的潜力后，他们回到荷兰，开始将单纯形法应用于炼油和石化行业的生产规划。数学优化有望帮助协调工业系统，因为这些系统中许多相互关联的决策很容易让单纯依靠人类推理变得力不从心 [4]。

However, the promise of linear programming came with a frustrating limitation. It worked beautifully when decisions could vary continuously, such as how much material to process, produce, or transport. Many important industrial decisions were not continuous. They required choosing an option or rejecting it, activating an alternative or leaving it unused, and answering yes or no. Introducing these discrete variables transformed an otherwise manageable linear program into a much harder mixed-variable optimization problem.
然而，线性规划的前景也伴随着一个令人沮丧的局限性。当决策可以连续变化时（例如处理、生产或运输多少材料），它表现得非常出色。但许多重要的工业决策并非连续的。它们需要选择或拒绝某个选项、启用或闲置某个方案，并回答“是”或“否”。引入这些离散变量，将原本可控的线性规划问题转化为了难度大得多的混合变量优化问题。

Benders focused precisely on this complication. In 1959, he and two Shell colleagues presented an algorithm for linear programs containing binary variables at a RAND symposium, reporting computational experiments performed on a Ferranti Mark I computer whose addition time was approximately 1.2 milliseconds [4].
本德斯正是专注于解决这一复杂问题。1959年，他和两位壳牌同事在兰德研讨会上提出了一种针对包含二进制变量的线性规划算法，并报告了在 Ferranti Mark I 计算机上进行的计算实验，该计算机的加法运算时间约为 1.2 毫秒 [4]。

Benders could have searched only for a faster way to solve the complete model, but instead he questioned whether the complete model needed to be solved all at once. The discrete variables created much of the difficulty, yet once their values were fixed, the remaining decisions often formed an ordinary linear program. This suggested a different strategy: isolate the complicating decisions, allow one problem to choose them, and let a second problem determine their operational consequences. Instead of treating the optimization model as an indivisible object, Benders divided it into parts that could communicate with and progressively improve one another [5, 6].
本德斯本可以只寻找一种更快求解完整模型的方法，但他反思了是否真的需要一次性求解整个模型。离散变量造成了大部分困难，但一旦这些变量的值固定，剩余的决策往往就构成了一个普通的线性规划问题。这启发了一种不同的策略：将复杂的决策孤立出来，让一个问题负责选择它们，而让第二个问题确定其运营后果。本德斯没有将优化模型视为一个不可分割的整体，而是将其拆分为可以相互通信并逐步改进的各个部分 [5, 6]。

This insight produced something more subtle than simply solving two smaller problems. The first problem, which we now call the master problem, proposes a high-level decision while initially possessing only an incomplete understanding of its true consequences. The subproblem evaluates that proposal and returns mathematical information showing where the master has been too optimistic. This information is converted into a new constraint, known as a Benders cut, and added to the master before it tries again. With each iteration, the master develops a more accurate representation of the operational reality hidden behind its decisions.
这一洞察产生了一种比单纯求解两个较小问题更微妙的方法。第一个问题（我们现在称之为“主问题”）提出一个高层决策，但最初对其实际后果的了解并不完整。子问题评估该提议，并返回数学信息，指出主问题在哪些方面过于乐观。这些信息被转化为一个新的约束条件，即“本德斯割平面”（Benders cut），并在主问题下一次尝试前加入其中。通过每一次迭代，主问题对其决策背后隐藏的运营现实的理解就越准确。

The idea became the foundation of Benders’s doctoral thesis, Partitioning in Mathematical Programming, completed at Utrecht University in 1960 under the supervision of Hans Freudenthal [4, 5]. In 1962, he published the method in the now-classic paper “Partitioning Procedures for Solving Mixed-Variables Programming Problems” [6].
这一思想成为了本德斯博士论文《数学规划中的划分》（Partitioning in Mathematical Programming）的基础，该论文于1960年在乌得勒支大学完成，导师是汉斯·弗罗伊登塔尔（Hans Freudenthal）[4, 5]。1962年，他在现已成为经典的论文《求解混合变量规划问题的划分程序》中发表了该方法 [6]。

More than six decades later, Benders decomposition remains one of the most influential decomposition techniques in mathematical optimization. It has been extended, strengthened, and successfully applied to facility location, transportation, supply-chain design, energy systems, stochastic programming, scheduling, and many other difficult optimization problems [7].
六十多年后的今天，本德斯分解法仍然是数学优化中最具影响力的分解技术之一。它已被扩展、强化，并成功应用于设施选址、交通运输、供应链设计、能源系统、随机规划、调度以及许多其他困难的优化问题中 [7]。

Benders decomposition has also become a staple in my own operations research toolkit, but I must admit that it took me considerably longer than it should have to understand it properly. Its central insight is elegant and intuitive, yet many learning materials introduce it through dense notation, extreme points, dual rays, and lengthy derivations before clearly explaining what the algorithm is trying to accomplish. As a result, a technique built around a remarkably simple conversation between two optimization problems can initially appear far more mysterious than it really is. This series of three articles is my attempt to provide the Benders decomposition tutorial I wish。
本德斯分解法也成为了我个人运筹学工具箱中的必备工具，但我必须承认，我花的时间比预想的要长得多才真正理解它。其核心思想优雅且直观，但许多学习资料在清晰解释算法目的之前，就通过密集的符号、极点、对偶射线和冗长的推导来介绍它。结果，一种围绕两个优化问题之间极其简单的对话而构建的技术，起初看起来比实际情况要神秘得多。这一系列三篇文章是我尝试提供的本德斯分解法教程，也是我曾经希望拥有的教程。
