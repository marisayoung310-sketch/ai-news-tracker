# The Religion of Speed / 速度崇拜

**29 May, 2026**
**2026年5月29日**

At some point, “moving fast” stopped being a practical concern and became a moral position. You see it damn near everywhere now. How fast can we ship this? How fast can we respond? How fast can we hire? How fast can we scale? How fast can we pivot? How fast can we get something, anything, in front of people so we can say we’re making progress?
不知从何时起，“快速行动”不再是一个实际的考量，而变成了一种道德立场。如今，你几乎随处可见这种现象。我们能多快发布产品？我们能多快做出响应？我们能多快完成招聘？我们能多快实现规模化？我们能多快完成转型？我们能多快把东西——任何东西——推到用户面前，好让我们宣称自己正在取得进展？

It’s treated like proof of seriousness. If you’re moving fast, you’re ambitious. If you’re cautious, you’re scared. If you ask to slow down and think, you’re blocking momentum. If you point out that the current plan has all the structural integrity of wet cardboard, you’re being negative.
这被视为一种“认真”的证明。如果你行动迅速，你就是有野心；如果你谨慎行事，你就是胆小怕事；如果你要求放慢脚步思考，你就是在阻碍势头；如果你指出当前的计划像湿纸板一样脆弱，你就是在散布负能量。

This is how a lot of bad work gets protected. Not because it’s good, useful, or even halfway thought through, but because it’s fast. Speed has become a kind of institutional narcotic. It gives people the feeling of motion without requiring the discipline of judgment.
这就是许多糟糕的工作得以被“保护”的原因。不是因为它们优秀、有用，甚至不是因为它们经过了深思熟虑，仅仅是因为它们“快”。速度已经成为一种机构性的麻醉剂。它给人一种正在前进的错觉，却不需要判断力的约束。

“Do not confuse motion and progress. A rocking horse keeps moving but does not make any progress.” — Alfred A. Montapert
“不要混淆‘移动’与‘进步’。摇摆木马一直在动，却没有任何进展。”——阿尔弗雷德·A·蒙塔珀特

Everyone gets to feel busy. Everyone gets to feel urgent. Everyone gets to point at the smoke coming off the machine and say, “See? We’re doing something.” The problem is that a lot of what gets called speed is just impatience with a different name.
每个人都觉得自己很忙，每个人都觉得自己很紧迫。每个人都可以指着机器冒出的烟说：“看，我们正在干活呢。”问题在于，许多被称为“速度”的东西，其实只是换了个名字的“急躁”。

Real speed exists. Real speed is what happens when the work is understood, the constraints are clear, the people involved know what they’re doing, and the decisions have been made cleanly enough that execution can happen without constant re-litigation. That kind of speed is rare.
真正的速度是存在的。真正的速度发生于：工作已被理解，约束条件清晰，参与者知道自己在做什么，决策足够明确，以至于执行过程中无需不断地重新争论。那种速度是罕见的。

Most speed is vague requirements, half-decisions, unexamined dependencies, missing context, and a room full of people quietly hoping the next person in the chain figures out what was never actually decided. Then, when the thing breaks, everyone acts surprised. Of course it broke. It was built under the assumption that thinking was the expensive part.
大多数所谓的“速度”，其实是模糊的需求、半吊子的决策、未经审视的依赖关系、缺失的背景信息，以及满屋子人都在默默祈祷链条上的下一个人能搞定那些从未真正定下来的事。然后，当事情搞砸时，每个人都表现得很惊讶。当然会搞砸，因为它的构建前提是“思考是昂贵的成本”。

This shows up constantly in the software industry, but software is often just the place where the wreckage becomes visible to most people. The same thing happens in operations, management, hiring, logistics, customer service, product development, and damn near everything else where people mistake activity for progress.
这种情况在软件行业屡见不鲜，但软件往往只是让这种“残骸”对大多数人可见的地方。同样的事情也发生在运营、管理、招聘、物流、客户服务、产品开发以及几乎所有人们将“忙碌”误认为“进步”的领域。

People rush through the part where understanding is supposed to happen, then spend ten times longer cleaning up the consequences. The original rushed work gets recorded as “fast.” The cleanup gets recorded as “unexpected.” The rework gets recorded as “iteration.” The confusion gets recorded as “alignment.” The preventable failure gets recorded as “learning.”
人们匆忙跳过本该进行理解的阶段，然后花费十倍的时间去清理后果。最初的仓促工作被记录为“快速”；清理工作被记录为“意外”；返工被记录为“迭代”；混乱被记录为“对齐”；可预防的失败被记录为“学习”。

This is how organizations end up with systems nobody trusts, processes nobody understands, meetings nobody wants, dashboards nobody believes, and workarounds that become load-bearing pieces of the business. Nobody set out to build that. They just kept choosing speed over comprehension, one small decision at a time, until the whole place became a series of monuments to things resembling productivity but having no actual productive worth.
这就是为什么组织最终会拥有没人信任的系统、没人理解的流程、没人想参加的会议、没人相信的仪表盘，以及成为业务“承重墙”的临时补丁。没人打算构建这些东西。他们只是在每一个小决策中不断选择速度而非理解，直到整个组织变成了一系列纪念碑——它们看起来像生产力，却没有任何实际的生产价值。

Then some poor bastard gets hired to “modernize” it, which usually means replacing the visible mess while leaving the original religion intact.
然后，某个倒霉蛋会被雇来“现代化”这些系统，这通常意味着替换掉表面上的混乱，却让原有的“速度崇拜”完好无损。

The funny part is that slowing down usually does not mean moving slowly. That’s the part people miss. Slowing down means refusing to skip the part where the work becomes legible. What are we actually trying to produce? Who depends on it? What breaks if this assumption is wrong? What do we already know? What are we pretending not to know? Where has this failed before? What has to be true for this to work?
有趣的是，放慢脚步通常并不意味着行动缓慢。这是人们忽略的一点。放慢脚步意味着拒绝跳过让工作变得清晰的环节。我们到底想产出什么？谁依赖它？如果这个假设错了，什么会崩溃？我们已经知道了什么？我们在假装不知道什么？它以前在哪里失败过？要让它成功，什么必须成立？

Boring, load-bearing questions that keep our work from turning into Waco. But those questions feel slow because they remove the little dopamine hit people get from motion. You can’t posture your way through them. You can’t hide inside urgency. You can’t turn the conversation into a PowerPoint deck and call it progress. You have to actually understand the work, which is the part a lot of people are trying to avoid.
这些枯燥但承重的提问，能防止我们的工作变成一场灾难。但这些问题让人感觉“慢”，因为它们剥夺了人们从忙碌中获得的多巴胺快感。你无法通过摆姿态来应付它们，无法躲在紧迫感背后，也无法把对话变成PPT就称之为进步。你必须真正理解工作，而这正是许多人试图逃避的部分。

Speed is attractive because it lets people stay slightly abstract. It lets them keep decisions fuzzy. It lets them avoid responsibility for details until those details become someone else’s emergency. And when the emergency arrives, speed becomes the answer again. Move faster. Fix faster. Hire faster. Replace faster. Ship faster. The same disease silently becomes the cure.
速度之所以诱人，是因为它让人保持一种模糊的状态。它让决策保持模糊，让人在细节变成别人的紧急事件之前逃避责任。而当紧急事件到来时，速度又成了答案。更快地行动，更快地修复，更快地招聘，更快地替换，更快地发布。同一种疾病，悄然变成了治愈它的良药。

There is a difference between urgency and haste. Urgency is appropriate when something matters and time is real. Haste is what happens when people want the emotional relief of action without the burden of clarity.
“紧迫感”与“仓促”是有区别的。当事情重要且时间紧迫时，紧迫感是恰当的。而仓促，则是人们想要行动带来的情感慰藉，却不想承担清晰思考的负担时所发生的事情。

"Make haste slowly." — Baltasar Gracián
“从容不迫。”——巴尔塔萨·格拉西安

The older I get, the less impressed I am by speed as a standalone virtue. I care whether the work holds up. I care whether the person doing it understands the shape of the problem. I care whether the decision survives contact with reality. I care whether we are creating fewer problems than we solve.
年纪越大，我越不觉得“速度”本身是一种美德。我关心工作是否经得起考验，关心执行者是否理解问题的本质，关心决策在接触现实后是否依然有效，关心我们创造的问题是否少于我们解决的问题。

That doesn’t require theatrical slowness. Nobody needs to sit around writing their own deployment infra for six months to prove they’re thoughtful. This isn’t an argument for dragging your feet or turning every decision into an excuse for yet-another-meeting. It’s an argument for respecting the work enough to do it properly the first time.
这并不需要表演式的缓慢。没人需要花六个月时间去写自己的部署基础设施来证明自己深思熟虑。这不是为了拖延时间，也不是为了把每个决策都变成开会的借口。这只是为了尊重工作，从而在第一次就把它做好。

Understand first, decide second, and only when you've done the proper due diligence, then you can/should move. When those steps get reversed, the bill always comes due. Sometimes the bill is a broken system. Sometimes it’s a burned-out team. Sometimes it’s a customer who quietly leaves. Sometimes it’s years of operational scar tissue that everyone works around because nobody has the patience to go back and understand what actually happened.
先理解，后决策；只有在完成了适当的尽职调查后，你才能（也应该）行动。当这些步骤被颠倒时，代价迟早会找上门来。有时代价是崩溃的系统，有时是精疲力竭的团队，有时是悄然离去的客户，有时是多年积累的运营伤疤——每个人都在绕着它走，因为没人有耐心回头去弄清楚到底发生了什么。

The Religion of Speed survives because it gives people a clean excuse. It lets them say the world is changing too fast, the market is too competitive, the customer is too demanding, the team is too small, the budget is too tight, and the window...
“速度崇拜”之所以能存活，是因为它给了人们一个完美的借口。它让他们可以说：世界变化太快、市场竞争太激烈、客户要求太苛刻、团队太小、预算太紧，以及窗口期……
