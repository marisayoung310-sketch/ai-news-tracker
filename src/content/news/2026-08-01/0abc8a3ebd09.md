Montana’s plan to become an experimental medical hub just pushed forward. As of this week in Montana, biotech companies whose drugs have been through preliminary testing—sometimes in as few as 10 healthy people—can pay $12,500 to apply to a newly established review board for approval. Once its treatment is rubber-stamped, the company can sell it via experimental treatment clinics, the first of which is likely to be up and running around the end of this year.
蒙大拿州成为实验性医疗中心的计划刚刚向前推进了一步。从本周开始，在蒙大拿州，那些药物已通过初步测试（有时仅在 10 名健康人身上进行过测试）的生物技术公司，可以支付 12,500 美元向新成立的审查委员会申请批准。一旦其治疗方案获得批准，该公司就可以通过实验性治疗诊所进行销售，首批诊所预计将于今年年底左右投入运营。

Montana’s latest right-to-try legislation is unique. Access to drugs is theoretically available to anyone who gives informed consent and can pay. For some, especially people in the longevity community, that’s a hopeful and exciting prospect. But to others, it’s unethical and dangerous. Read our story to learn about where this may all be headed. —Jessica Hamzelou
蒙大拿州最新的“尝试权”立法非常独特。理论上，任何签署知情同意书并能支付费用的人都可以获得这些药物。对于一些人，尤其是长寿社区的人来说，这是一个充满希望且令人兴奋的前景。但对另一些人而言，这是不道德且危险的。阅读我们的报道，了解这一切可能走向何方。——Jessica Hamzelou

Montana’s new “right to try” law can’t come soon enough for some. Kris DeVault is desperate. His son, Brody, born in March 2023, has something called creatine transporter deficiency—a rare condition in which the brain and muscles lack the energy they need to develop. There are no cures for Brody’s condition. But DeVault has learned of a company developing a drug that might help. That drug is still in the early stages of development and has only been tested in animals and a small number of healthy adults. Doctors can’t prescribe it. DeVault knows the drug might not work. But he’s doing all he can to access it regardless. Read our story about DeVault’s efforts. —Jessica Hamzelou
对于一些人来说，蒙大拿州新的“尝试权”法律出台得越快越好。Kris DeVault 心急如焚。他出生于 2023 年 3 月的儿子 Brody 患有一种名为“肌酸转运蛋白缺乏症”的罕见疾病，导致大脑和肌肉缺乏发育所需的能量。目前 Brody 的病情尚无治愈方法。但 DeVault 得知有一家公司正在开发一种可能有帮助的药物。该药物仍处于早期开发阶段，仅在动物和少数健康成年人身上进行过测试。医生无法开具处方。DeVault 知道这种药可能无效，但他仍竭尽全力试图获取它。阅读我们关于 DeVault 努力的报道。——Jessica Hamzelou

Congress used to evaluate emerging technologies. Let’s do it again. The US Office of Technology Assessment (OTA), an independent office created by Congress in the early 1970s, produced some 750 reports during its 23-year history, assessing technologies as varied as electronic surveillance, genetic engineering, and remote sensing from outer space. The office functioned like a debunking arm. It sussed out the snake oil. Its reports saw through the alluring gleam of overhyped technologies. Since its unceremonious defunding in 1995, perennial calls have gone out: Rouse the office from the dead! Read our story about why, with advances in robotics, big data, and AI systems, these calls have taken on a new level of urgency. —Peter Andrey Smith
国会曾经评估过新兴技术，让我们重拾这一做法。美国国会技术评估办公室（OTA）是国会在 20 世纪 70 年代初设立的一个独立机构，在其 23 年的历史中撰写了约 750 份报告，评估了从电子监控、基因工程到太空遥感等各种技术。该办公室发挥着“辟谣”部门的作用，它能识破“江湖骗术”，其报告能看穿被过度炒作的技术那诱人的光环。自 1995 年被草率地停止拨款以来，人们不断呼吁：让这个办公室起死回生吧！阅读我们的报道，了解为什么随着机器人、大数据和 AI 系统的进步，这些呼声变得愈发紧迫。——Peter Andrey Smith
