### How lasers could help provide fuel for nuclear reactors
### 激光技术如何助力核反应堆燃料供应

Nuclear power provides about 9% of global electricity today, and that fraction could tick up as countries look to build new reactors. New, cheaper methods to obtain fuel could help ensure that those nuclear projects stay on track. One of those methods is called laser enrichment. It allows you to separate out the material you want (in this case, uranium) from others in a mixture of old waste. A company called Global Laser Enrichment (GLE) is about to start testing whether the technology works at commercial scale. Read our story about their efforts. —Casey Crownhart
核能目前提供了全球约 9% 的电力，随着各国寻求建造新的反应堆，这一比例可能会上升。获取燃料的更廉价新方法有助于确保这些核项目顺利进行。其中一种方法被称为激光浓缩。它能让你从旧废料混合物中分离出所需的材料（在本例中为铀）。一家名为“全球激光浓缩”（GLE）的公司即将开始测试该技术是否能在商业规模上运作。阅读我们关于他们工作的报道。——Casey Crownhart

### The quest to keep organs alive outside the body
### 让器官在体外存活的探索

It’s super difficult to freeze organs. Once ice forms in them, they’re done. The ice crystals create all kinds of damage and render the organs unusable. That hasn’t stopped many researchers from trying. In new research, one team has been able to supercool the kidneys of pigs and preserve them for days. The kidneys survived being stored at −4 °C (25 °F) and eventually reimplanted back into pigs. And that’s just the latest development in a field that is positively buzzing. Read about why it’s such an exciting time for organ preservation—and what could be coming next. —Jessica Hamzelou
冷冻器官极其困难。一旦器官内形成冰晶，它们就报废了。冰晶会造成各种损害，使器官无法使用。但这并没有阻止许多研究人员的尝试。在一项新研究中，一个团队成功地对猪的肾脏进行了过冷处理，并保存了数天。这些肾脏在 -4°C (25°F) 的环境下储存后存活下来，并最终被重新植入猪体内。这只是该领域最新的一项进展，目前该领域正处于活跃期。阅读关于为何现在是器官保存领域激动人心的时刻，以及未来可能的发展。——Jessica Hamzelou
